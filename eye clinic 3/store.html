Clinical Guide

How to Read Your Optical Prescription Slip

Optical prescriptions written by Dr. Muhammad Waqas or other ophthalmologists follow standardized international abbreviations:

OD (Oculus Dexter)

Latin term for your Right Eye. Always listed on the top row of your prescription matrix.

OS (Oculus Sinister)

Latin term for your Left Eye. Always listed on the second row of your prescription matrix.

SPH (Sphere)

Measures lens power needed for distance or reading. Negative numbers (−) indicate nearsightedness; positive (+) indicate farsightedness.

CYL & Axis

Corrects astigmatism (corneal curvature irregularity). The Axis (1°–180°) identifies the orientation of the astigmatic correction.

ADD (Near Add)

Additional magnifying power required for reading in progressive or bifocal lenses, typically prescribed after age 40 for presbyopia.

PD (Pupillary Distance)

The distance between the centers of your pupils in millimeters. Ensures the optical center of the lens aligns precisely with your line of sight.

Single Vision

One focal power across the whole lens. Perfect for people who need glasses solely for distance, reading, or computer work.

Progressive Lenses

Modern multifocal lenses with no visible line on the lens surface. Lets you see near, intermediate (computer), and far distances seamlessly.
